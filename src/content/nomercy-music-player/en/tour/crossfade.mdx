# Crossfade

**Crossfade is already on.** The music player sets `crossfadeEnabled: true` before it reads your config, so a queue that runs to the end of a track overlaps into the next one without you asking.

That is a music-only default. The kit underneath defaults it to `false`, and the video player leaves it there.

## The window

Two values decide the overlap, and the fade spans both.

| Field | Default | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `crossfadeLeadSeconds` | `3` | How far before the end of the outgoing track the fade starts. |
| `crossfadeTailSeconds` | `3` | How far into the incoming track it keeps going. |

Six seconds of overlap in total, three either side of the join.

```ts
player.setup({
  crossfadeLeadSeconds: 6,
  crossfadeTailSeconds: 6,
  ...
});
```

**Raising the lead eats the end of the outgoing track, not the gap between them.** There is no gap. The fade is an overlap, so a longer lead means the outgoing track is already quietening while more of it is still playing.

## The shape of the fade

`equal-power` is the default. It holds the combined loudness steady across the join, which is what stops the middle of a crossfade sounding like a dip.

`linear` is the other option. Both tracks move at a constant rate, so the middle is quieter, and it is the right choice when the two tracks are meant to sound like one continuous take.

```ts
player.setup({
  crossfadeDefaults: {
    curve: 'linear',
  },
  ...
});
```

## Turning it off

```ts
player.setup({
  crossfadeEnabled: false,
  ...
});
```

Tracks then cut straight from one to the next.

## Crossfading on your own terms

The automatic path handles a track reaching its end. When you want the change to happen for another reason, a listener pressing next or your own radio logic choosing something, call it directly:

```ts
await player.crossfadeTo(nextTrack, {
  duration: 8,
});
```

**You should now hear the current track fade down as the new one comes up**, over eight seconds, rather than the queue's own three.

`duration` is in seconds. Leave it out and it falls back to `crossfadeDefaults.duration`, then to `5`.

`curve` takes the same two values as above. `startAt` begins the incoming track partway in, and it is in **milliseconds** rather than seconds, which is the one place the two units differ.

Two failures throw rather than fading:

- An item with no `url` throws, because there is nothing to fade into.
- A backend that reports no crossfade support throws. Both shipped backends support it, so this only happens behind a backend of your own.

**These are two separate mechanisms, and that is deliberate.** The automatic one is a property of the queue and fires on its own schedule. `crossfadeTo` is a method you call, at a moment only your application knows about. Leaving both on is normal, and they do not interfere: the automatic fade only ever runs at the end of a track.
