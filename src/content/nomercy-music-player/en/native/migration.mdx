---
title: Migrating an app to the native player
description: Moving a Kotlin or Swift app off its own embedded audio player.
---

Most apps arrive here with an audio player already in them: a Media3 session
wired into a service, or an AVAudioPlayer with a hand-drawn bar. The move is not
a rewrite. The engine you have keeps decoding; what changes is who owns the
queue, the crossfade and the state the bar reads.

## What to delete

Anything tracking whether playback is running, anything holding the queue and
its cursor, anything deciding what plays next under shuffle or repeat, and
anything converting a position into a scrubber fraction. Those four are where
two clients of one server drift apart, and the library holds one answer for each.

Keep your engine setup, your data source, your auth headers and your media
session.

## Kotlin

Wrap the engine you already build.

```kotlin
val player = NMMusicPlayer(backend)
```

Where your screen read the engine directly, read `player.state()` or collect
`player.stateFlow`. Where it advanced the queue itself, call `player.next()` and
let the announcement reach the bar, the full screen and the notification at once.

Swap the chrome last. `rememberMusicChrome` gives you the state and the commands
the shipped surfaces take, and a screen can mount `MiniPlayer` beside its own
while you move one control at a time.

## Swift

The SwiftUI surfaces take the same state object the Compose ones do, bridged
through the framework. An app conforms what it has and gets `MiniPlayer` and
`FullPlayerScreen` over it.

## What you gain by moving

Plugins, and one queue. Scrobbling stops being a hook inside your playback code
and becomes something that registers and unregisters cleanly; lyrics stop being
a screen that polls the engine and become something that listens. And a queue
that lives in the player rather than in a screen is a queue the lock screen, the
car and a cast target can all read the same way.
