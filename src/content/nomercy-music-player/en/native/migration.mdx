---
title: Migrating an app to the native player
description: Moving a Kotlin or Swift app off its own embedded audio player.
---

Most apps arrive here with an audio player already in them: a Media3 session
wired into a service, or an AVAudioPlayer with a hand-drawn bar. The move is not
a rewrite. The engine you have keeps decoding. What changes is who owns the
queue, the crossfade, and the state the bar reads.

## What to delete

Delete anything that:

- tracks whether playback is running
- holds the queue and its cursor
- decides what plays next under shuffle or repeat
- converts a position into a scrubber fraction

Those four are where two clients of one server drift apart. The library holds
one answer for each.

Keep your engine setup, your data source, your auth headers and your media
session.

## Kotlin

Wrap the engine you already build.

```kotlin
val player = NMMusicPlayer(backend)
```

Two call sites change when you wrap the engine:

| Your code | Replace it with |
| --- | --- |
| Reads the engine's state directly | `player.state()`, or collect `player.stateFlow` |
| Advances the queue itself | Call `player.next()`, which reaches the bar, the full screen, and the notification at once |

Swap the chrome last. `rememberMusicChrome` gives you the state and the commands
the shipped surfaces take, and a screen can mount `MiniPlayer` beside its own
while you move one control at a time.

## Swift

The SwiftUI surfaces take the same state object the Compose ones do, bridged
through the framework. An app conforms what it has and gets `MiniPlayer` and
`FullPlayerScreen` over it.

## What you gain by moving

You gain plugins, and one queue. Two examples of what changes:

| Feature | Without the library | With the library |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scrobbling | A hook inside your playback code | Something that registers and unregisters cleanly |
| Lyrics | A screen that polls the engine | Something that listens |

A queue that lives in the player, rather than in a screen, is one the lock
screen, the car, and a cast target all read the same way.
