# NAS

A NAS runs NoMercy through Docker. Synology, QNAP, Unraid and TrueNAS all provide a way to run a container, and NoMercy is a normal container.

Read the [Docker page](/nomercy-media-server/installation/docker) first. This page covers what is different on a NAS.

## Check the processor first

Images are built for `linux/amd64` only.

Many NAS units, especially smaller Synology and QNAP models, run an ARM processor. Check your model's specification before going further.

## Paths inside the container

Your NAS presents your media at a path like `/volume1/media`. The container sees whatever path you mount it to.

Every path you enter in NoMercy afterwards is the path **inside** the container. If you mount `/volume1/media` to `/media`, then in NoMercy your library folder is under `/media`.

This trips up almost everyone once.

A NAS holds its media locally, so mount the volume into the container and use a **Local** driver in NoMercy. The NFS and SMB drivers are for reaching a share the machine cannot mount, which is not the case here.

## Permissions

The container needs to read your media, and it needs to write into the library folders you give it.

If a scan finds nothing, or importing fails immediately, check the ownership of those folders on the NAS before looking anywhere else.

## Completing setup

There is no browser on the NAS. Browse to `http://<your-nas-ip>:7626/setup` from your computer.

The container log also prints a sign-in code and a link you can open on your phone.
