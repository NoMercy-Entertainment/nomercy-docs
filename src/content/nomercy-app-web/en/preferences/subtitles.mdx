# Subtitle style

How subtitles are drawn, set once and used everywhere. The screen shows a preview as you change it, so you can see the result instead of imagining it.

## What you can set

**Font.** ReithSans, Arial, Courier New, Georgia or Verdana.

**Size.** As a proportion, so it scales with the screen rather than being fixed to one.

**Text colour and opacity.**

**Background colour and opacity.** The box behind the words. Fully transparent by default.

**Edge style.** How the letters are separated from the picture: none, drop shadow, text shadow, raised, depressed or uniform outline. A shadow or an outline is what keeps white text readable over a bright scene.

**Window colour and opacity.** The band behind the whole subtitle area, rather than behind the words.

## Where it applies

The style is saved on this device and used by the player here. Setting it on the phone leaves the television as it was.

## Subtitles that style themselves

Some subtitles carry their own styling: positioned signs, songs, and anything designed to sit against the picture. Those are drawn as their author intended and keep their own look.

Your settings apply to plain subtitles, which is nearly all dialogue.
