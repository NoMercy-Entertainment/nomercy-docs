# Ripper

The ripper screen walks a disc from the tray into your library. Watching a disc without ripping it is a different, simpler screen; this one is for keeping it.

## The drives

Every drive on the machine is listed with what is in it: empty, tray open, or a disc ready. The list keeps itself current, so putting a disc in updates the screen without a refresh.

The tray can be opened and closed from here, which saves walking over to the machine.

## Identifying the disc

The server probes the disc and proposes what it is. Where it is unsure it offers candidates with how confident it is in each, and you pick.

Nothing is written until you agree. If none of the candidates is right, search for the title yourself and assign it.

## Choosing what to rip

A disc holds many titles, most of them menus, trailers and warnings. The main feature is marked, and each title shows its length so a wrong pick is obvious.

For the titles you keep, choose the audio and subtitle tracks by language. Forced subtitle tracks are marked, since those carry the translated signs rather than the dialogue.

## Where it lands

Pick the library and the folder, and the encoding profile to use. The default profile is used when you do not choose one.

## Ripping

Starting a rip queues it. Progress shows against the drive, and the screen can be left; the work is on the server, not in the page.

::Troubleshooting

**A disc reports protection.** The screen says so rather than failing quietly part way through.

**No drives listed.** The machine has none the server can see. On Linux this is usually the service's access to the device.

**Identification finds nothing.** Search for the title and assign it by hand. A rip with a manual assignment is as good as an automatic one.

::
