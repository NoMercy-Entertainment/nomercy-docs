# Core: The Queue

`NMMusicPlayer` and `NMVideoPlayer` don't maintain their own array of playlist items with a separate cursor variable. They delegate to one `MediaList<T>` instance each, and every queue method on the player is a thin wrapper over it.

## MediaList

`MediaList<T extends BasePlaylistItem>` is an ordered list that knows where the cursor is.

- The whole array: `get()`, `set(items)`
- The cursor: `current()`, `currentIndex()`, `setCurrent(target)`
- Reading without moving it: `peekNext()`, `peekPrevious()`
- Mutations: `append`, `prepend`, `insert`, `remove`, `removeAt`, `move`, `clear`, `shuffle`, `sort`

`setCurrent` takes an item reference, its `id`, a numeric index, or a predicate. No caller has to resolve an index first.

Every mutation that shifts positions shifts the cursor with it, so it keeps pointing at the same logical item.

Remove the item before the current one and the cursor decrements. Shuffle, and it re-finds the playing item's new position and follows it.

Every mutation emits a typed event: `change`, `append`, `current` and so on. The player mixin re-emits those on the public bus as `queue`, `queue:append`, `item` and the rest, so a UI never polls.

Two lists exist per player: `_queueList` for what's coming, and `_backlogList` for what already played, so `previous()` can look back past the queue head without the primary list growing unbounded in the wrong direction.

## The player-facing surface

The `queue*` methods all delegate straight to `_queueList`, wiring its events to the public bus on first use: `queue()`, `queueAppend()`, `queuePrepend()`, `queueInsert()`, `queueRemove()`, `queueRemoveAt()`, `queueMove()`, `queueClear()`, `queueShuffle()`, `queueSort()`.

`item(target)` moves the cursor. Once the player is past `setup`, it also kicks off a `load()` for the resolved item.

These read with no side effects: `peekNext()`, `peekPrevious()`, `queueLength()`, `queueIndexOf(id)`, `index()`.

`playItem(target)` and `playNow(items, start?)` exist because `queue(items); item(start); play()` races: `item()` fires a fire-and-forget `load()`, and a `play()` called right after it can reach the backend before the source is even set. Both helpers route through `item(target, { autoplay: true })` internally, which only calls `play()` inside the resolved `load()`'s own continuation, never before it.

Every entry ingested by `queue()` / `queueAppend()` / `queuePrepend()` / `queueInsert()` passes through a normalization step first: your player class's optional `normalizePlaylistItem` (for accepting a legacy wire format), then the consumer's `transformPlaylistItem` config callback, then title-token interpolation for any tokens your player registered via `registerTitleTokens()`.
