# What is the NoMercy Video Player

[`nomercy-video-player`](/nomercy-video-player/api/factory) is the headless, event-driven HTML5 video player that powers video on NoMercy TV. It plays standard video through the browser's native `<video>` element, and adds adaptive HLS streaming (via hls.js) when you point it at an HLS source. The playback backend is a swappable adapter, so other streaming formats can plug in the same way.

It handles the genuinely hard parts for you — matching HDR to the display, rendering subtitles in several formats, switching bitrate as the network shifts — and then gets out of your way.

The important word is *headless*. There is no UI in the box and no layout you have to accept: you get a working video pipeline wired to plain events and methods, and everything on screen is yours to build — or you bring in the optional [`DesktopUiPlugin`](/nomercy-video-player/plugins/desktop-ui) and start from a finished one.

## What you get

- `NMVideoPlayer<T>`, created through the [`nmplayer(id)`](/nomercy-video-player/api/factory) factory and generic over your own playlist item type.
- Plays standard video natively, and adds adaptive HLS streaming with HDR-aware quality switching (via hls.js) when the source is HLS.
- Multi-format subtitles (VTT, ASS/SSA), chapters, and skip markers.
- Chromecast, a full keyboard and touch input layer, and a fully typed [event map](/nomercy-video-player/events).
- The optional [`DesktopUiPlugin`](/nomercy-video-player/plugins/desktop-ui) for a finished UI, or build your own from events.
- Swappable [adapter ports](/nomercy-video-player/adapters/video-backend) — backend, thumbnail, chapter, and subtitle sources, and more — replaceable without subclassing.
- [Container state classes](/nomercy-video-player/state-classes) for styling around playback in pure CSS.
- Full TypeScript types for every method, event, and option.

## Where it sits

The generic parts (queue, auth, plugins, i18n, storage) live in `nomercy-player-core` underneath, so this package stays focused on the hard video parts. Plugin authors type against `IVideoPlayer<T>` rather than the concrete class.
