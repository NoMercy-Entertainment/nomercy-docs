---
title: Migrating an app to the native player
description: Moving a Kotlin or Swift app off its own embedded player.
---

Most apps arrive here with a player already in them: an ExoPlayer wired directly
into a screen, or an AVPlayer with hand-drawn controls. The move is not a
rewrite. The engine you have keeps decoding; what changes is who owns the state
above it and who draws the controls.

## What to delete

The parts worth removing are the ones this library already answers for, and the
list is longer than it looks. Anything tracking whether playback is running,
anything converting a position into a progress fraction, anything remembering a
selected subtitle across a track change, and anything deciding when the controls
hide. Those are the four places two clients of the same server drift apart, and
they are the four the library holds one answer for.

Keep your engine setup, your data source, your auth headers and your surface.

## Kotlin

Wrap the engine you already build rather than replacing it.

```kotlin
val backend = ExoPlayerVideoBackend(context)
val player = NMVideoPlayer(backend)
```

`NMVideoPlayer` is a `ComposedPlayer`, so everything the core reference lists is
on it. Where your screen read `exoPlayer.isPlaying`, read `player.state()` or
collect `player.stateFlow`. Where it called `exoPlayer.seekTo(ms)`, call
`player.time(seconds)` and let the announcement reach anything else listening.

Swap your controls last. `NMVideoPlayerView` takes the player and an optional
surface, and a screen can mount it beside its own chrome while you move one
control at a time.

## Swift

Conform what you have to `VideoEngine` rather than adopting a new engine. It is
seven members: the `AVPlayer`, an observe callback, three transport calls and
three selection calls. Hand that to `EngineVideoPlayer` and the chrome works
over it.

The engine adapter for the Kotlin core is not shipped yet. Until it is, an
Apple app conforms its own AVFoundation code to `VideoEngine` and gets the full
chrome over it, which is the path the quickstart shows.

## What you gain by moving

Plugins. Once the player owns the state, a feature is a plugin rather than a
change to a screen: it registers, listens, contributes UI, and unregisters
cleanly. The
[plugin scaffold](https://github.com/NoMercy-Entertainment/nomercy-player-core-kmp/tree/master/templates/plugin)
is a working one to copy, and the shipped testing kit tests it without a device.
